CHAPTER 02 • BIOENERGETICS

The Science of Body Recomposition

"Can you build muscle and lose fat simultaneously? Peer-reviewed exercise physiology confirms: an unequivocal yes."

⚡ The Energy Flux Equation

Muscle tissue and adipose (fat) tissue are two independent metabolic compartments governed by different biochemical pathways:

  • Muscle Protein Synthesis (MPS): The cellular assembly of dietary amino acids into new contractile myofibrillar proteins (actin and myosin).
  • Lipolysis: The breakdown of stored triglycerides in adipose tissue into free fatty acids and glycerol for cellular fuel.

Synthesizing 1 kg of muscle requires roughly 5,000 to 7,500 kcal of metabolic work. At 85 kg, your body holds tens of thousands of calories in stored adipose fat. In a mild 12–15% deficit (-300 kcal), your adipose tissue acts as an endogenous battery pack, mobilizing fatty acids to bridge the caloric deficit and supply the chemical energy needed for ribosomal protein synthesis.

🧬 Net Protein Balance: MPS vs. MPB

Net Protein Balance = Muscle Protein Synthesis (MPS) - Muscle Protein Breakdown (MPB)

Whenever MPS exceeds MPB over a 24-hour cycle, muscle hypertrophy occurs. Progressive mechanical tension stimulates the mTORC1 pathway, and consuming 180g of high-quality protein provides the necessary essential amino acid substrate.

🧪 The Leucine Threshold (~3.0g Leucine / Meal)
Muscle Protein Synthesis operates on an all-or-none switch mechanism. Reaching the ~3.0g leucine threshold requires approximately 40g–45g of complete protein per feeding. Eating 4 distinct feedings spaced 3.5–5 hours apart turns on the mTORC1 switch multiple times per day.

⚠️ Why Severe Deficits Destroy Recomposition

Crash dieting (>500 kcal deficit) activates AMPK (the cellular energy depletion sensor). AMPK directly inhibits mTORC1, shutting down protein synthesis, dropping daytime testosterone by 15%, and causing muscle wasting. A modest deficit of -300 kcal protects hormones while burning fat.
